Sampling Check

A sampling plan has to survive three questions: how much you can actually code in the hours you have, how many units the reliability sample needs, and how large a difference a sample that size could even detect. This page answers all three from your own numbers.

Nothing is uploaded. The arithmetic runs in this page, on your machine. No account, no server, no network request once the page has loaded.

How these numbers are produced

  • The time estimate charges full price for the first variable and 60 percent for each one after it, because the coder reads the unit once, then holds back a fifth of the total for the second reliability pass and the disagreement discussion. Both assumptions are stated so you can argue with them in your methods section.
  • The reliability sample is the larger of 50 units, 10 percent of the corpus capped at 300, and whatever it takes for your rarest category to appear about ten times. The rare category is usually what drives it, and it is the part students miss.
  • The detectable effect uses the normal approximation for a two-group comparison and the noncentral chi-square for the crosstab. It is a planning figure, accurate enough to tell you whether a sample is in the right order of magnitude.
  • Decide all of this before you look at the data. A sample size chosen after a result is not a sampling plan.
