Elaboration Likelihood Model

ELM · proposed by Richard Petty and John Cacioppo, 1986

Persuasion is not one process but two, and which one fires depends on how much a person is willing and able to think about the message in front of them.

The model draws a line between a central route and a peripheral route. On the central route, an audience scrutinizes the actual argument, weighs the evidence, and forms an attitude that tends to stick. On the peripheral route, people lean on shortcuts: the attractiveness of the source, the sheer number of arguments, or a pleasant mood standing in for real reasoning.

Why it matters for health messaging

A campaign that wants durable behavior change has to push audiences toward the central route, which means motivation and ability both need to be high. When either is low, peripheral cues take over and the resulting attitude is easier to reverse.

Teaching example

A vaccination flyer dense with statistics will only persuade a reader who cares enough to process it. For a distracted reader, a trusted physician's photograph may do more work than the data ever could.
